## Assumptions and progression

1. Ch1 introduces weak bath coupling, large heat capacity and finite normalisers. Measures and coordinate Jacobians precede every sampling claim. NVE preservation is distinguished from ergodic sampling.
2. Ch2 introduces physical model approximation independently of numerical error. Smooth derivatives are used only for smooth potentials; hard spheres are constraints/impulses. Long-range boundary conventions are explicit.
3. Ch3 introduces stochastic dynamics with constant isotropic friction and white-noise approximation. The overdamped model is a timescale limit. Stationarity uses valid boundaries. Numerical bias and initialisation precede production averages.
4. Ch4 introduces independent estimation before chains. Finite-state ergodic statements are separated from countable/continuous hypotheses, distributional convergence from time averages. Metropolis ratios always include proposal asymmetry where relevant.
5. Ch5 introduces stationary finite covariance accounting before asymptotic CLT/ESS. Negative correlations, finite-size RDF baseline, kinetic heat-capacity contribution and physical transport time prevent overgeneralisation.
6. Ch6 applies, rather than reintroduces, the Ising energy/local move. Its new result is the joint spin–bond construction and cluster balance proof; its performance discussion uses Ch5's observable/cost language.
7. Ch7 extends residence probability to exact waiting-time sampling, then introduces lifting and factor hazards. Global balance is proved for translation-invariant pair factors; no arbitrary many-body lifting claim.
8. Ch8 changes particle interpretation explicitly. Kernel smoothing and particle quadrature are distinct approximations; conservative SPH may derive from continuum internal energy, though not microscopic pair potentials.
9. Ch9 applies the partition-function and measure conventions to reduced marginals, coordinate changes and altered sampling. Static-bias identities are not used on arbitrary time-dependent histories. FEP/TI require common reference factors, support and convergence.

## Recurring representations

- Fixed energy-level example: one ground state, four excited states. Ch1 shows probabilities; Ch9 reinterprets the same weights as entropy/free energy.
- Harmonic models: Ch3 supplies exact invariant width and numerical bias; Ch7 uses a clearly different harmonic _pair factor_ for event timing; Ch9 checks free-energy differences analytically. Their parameters and purposes are stated separately.
- Probability flow: Ch4 detailed balance → Ch6 construction probabilities → Ch7 global balance in an enlarged space. Stationarity is recalled, not rederived from elementary probability each time.
- Teal is the first or active quantity, amber its labelled comparator; stroke patterns and text repeat the distinction. In the event figure teal tracks activity, not permanent particle identity. Every figure states its units/scales and illustrative status.
- Figures use existing responsive two-panel story grids, stack in reading order, and retain the same explanatory content without JavaScript. Two registered PredictReveal uses have no-score prediction, reset and no-script explanations.
