## Editorial assessment of the implemented chapter

The original jumps from a model/misfit definition to a catalogue of linear-algebra facts. The revision gives those facts a shared purpose: identifying the nearest attainable prediction. The initial calibration is solved algebraically, then revisited geometrically after projection has been constructed. The second encounter explains the first instead of merely repeating its answer. The figure is introduced through a prediction, interpreted immediately afterwards, formalised by reconstruction/Parseval, and reused through its calibration preset later.

The chapter retains named definitions, the Hermitian spectral theorem, Gram positivity, matrix square roots and Cholesky, with anchor navigation. It adds explicit proofs of reconstruction, Parseval, closest-point optimality, eigenvalue positivity and uniqueness of the principal SPSD square root. It states the spectral and Cholesky existence theorems without pretending the figure proves them. Physical assumptions, complex conjugation conventions and the difference between parameter uniqueness and predicted-vector uniqueness are explicit.

Corrections to original source: stress versus force and strain versus extension are distinguished; “linear in parameters” replaces the misleading statistical-GLM label with an explanatory sidenote; infinite Fourier sums are deferred until convergence can be specified; rank one is qualified as rank at most one; SPD Cholesky uses lower triangular `LL*` and positive diagonal, with a counterexample to blanket SPSD uniqueness. These are correctness repairs, not simplifications to fit the figure.
